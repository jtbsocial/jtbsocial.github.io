The Origin Story: From Fortnite Clips to Viral Supremacy

Born on March 28, 2005, David Anthony Burke spent much of his early youth moving between Queens, New York, and Houston, Texas. Homeschooled during his teenage years, Burke poured thousands of hours into competitive gaming—specifically Fortnite. As he built a dedicated YouTube channel publishing high-octane montage edits, he ran into a persistent obstacle: copyright strikes.

Every time he used trending hip-hop or indie tracks behind his gameplay, record labels flagged his content. His mother gave him a straightforward challenge: If you don't want copyright issues, why don't you make the music yourself?

Taking that advice literally, 16-year-old Burke downloaded BandLab—a free, cloud-based digital audio workstation (DAW) for mobile devices—and began recording vocal melodies into Apple wired earbuds inside his sister's bedroom closet for acoustic isolation. He didn't know music theory, chord structures, or audio engineering. He operated strictly by ear, blending moody indie-rock guitar riffs found online with raw, reverb-drenched vocal takes.
