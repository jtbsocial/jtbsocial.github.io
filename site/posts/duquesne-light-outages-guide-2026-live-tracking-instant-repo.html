3. The Restoration Workflow: How Duquesne Light Prioritizes Grid Repairs

When widespread severe weather strikes Western Pennsylvania—such as summer microbursts or heavy ice storms—thousands of power restoration requests flood the system simultaneously. Duquesne Light follows a strict priority protocol designed to maximize safety and restore power to critical infrastructure first.

                [1. Public Safety Hazards] (Downed Lines, Fires)
                           │
                [2. Critical Infrastructure] (Hospitals, Water Plants, Emergency Services)
                           │
                [3. Transmission Lines & Substations] (High-Voltage Backbone)
                           │
                [4. Primary Distribution Circuits] (Restores 1,000+ Customers)
                           │
                [5. Local Branch Lines & Transformers] (Restores 10–50 Customers)
                           │
                [6. Individual Service Drops] (Restores Single Homes)

Restoration Priority Breakdown

  • Phase 1: Public Safety Hazards: Teams clear live downed wires, fallen high-voltage lines blocking major roadways, and hazardous electrical conditions.
  • Phase 2: Critical Infrastructure: Priority routing goes to medical centers, water treatment facilities, communications towers, and emergency response centers.
  • Phase 3: High-Voltage Transmission Infrastructure: Large transmission lines feed thousands of homes via localized substations. Repairing these restores energy to the largest number of customers simultaneously.
  • Phase 4: Distribution Feeders: Main neighborhood feeder circuits are cleared of fallen branches or shorted transformers.
  • Phase 5: Neighborhood Service Drops: Individual service connections from utility poles to home weatherheads are repaired last.

4. 2026 Smart Grid Technologies: FLISR, AMI, and Predictive AI

Duquesne Light has modernized its infrastructure across its 800-square-mile service territory. These technology deployments reduce outage frequency and shorten downtime during unexpected events.

FLISR (Fault Location, Isolation, and Service Restoration)

FLISR is the backbone of DLC's self-healing power grid. When a tree branch hits a power line, automated reclosers and digital switches instantly locate the failure, isolate the damaged line segment, and reroute power from alternative substations—all in under two minutes without requiring manual operator action.

Advanced Metering Infrastructure 2.0 (AMI)

Second-generation smart meters provide two-way telemetry between homes and DLC's control network. These devices send automated "last gasp" wireless signals to utility servers the millisecond voltage drops to zero, pin-pointing line breaks instantly.

AI Predictive Maintenance

Using satellite imagery, LiDAR mapping, and AI algorithms, Duquesne Light continuously monitors vegetation growth near high-voltage lines. This proactive approach identifies high-risk trees before severe weather causes unexpected branch failures.

6. Severe Weather & Seasonal Power Outages in Western PA

Southwestern Pennsylvania experiences seasonal storm profiles that present unique challenges to electrical distribution:

Winter Ice & Heavy Snow

  • Risk Factor: Ice accumulation exceeding 0.25 inches adds hundreds of pounds of weight to tree branches and overhead lines.
  • Action Item: Ensure weatherheads (the pipe where power enters your home) are inspected regularly. If damaged, repairs must be conducted by a licensed electrician before Duquesne Light can reattach service cables.

Summer Severe Thunderstorms & Microbursts

  • Risk Factor: Sudden straight-line winds exceeding 60 mph knock down mature oak and maple trees onto main distribution feeders.
  • Action Item: Report transformer sparks, loud popping sounds, or tree limbs resting against wires immediately through the online portal.

Generator Safety Guidelines

  • Never operate gas generators indoors, inside garages, or near open windows due to dangerous carbon monoxide buildup.
  • Install a transfer switch if connecting a generator directly to your circuit breaker box to prevent dangerous backfeeding into Duquesne Light utility lines, which can endanger line crews.

8. Damage Claims & Appliance Protection Guidelines

Voltage fluctuations, power surges, and sudden blackouts can damage sensitive household electronics like gaming rigs, smart appliances, and televisions.

Duquesne Light Damage Claim Policies

Duquesne Light evaluates damage claims based on tariff regulations established by the Pennsylvania Public Utility Commission (PAPUC). Generally, utility companies are not liable for property damages caused by weather events, acts of nature, or emergency shutdowns required for public safety.

Protecting Your Smart Home Tech

  1. Whole-Home Surge Protectors: Install a Type 1 or Type 2 Surge Protective Device (SPD) inside your main breaker panel to suppress high-voltage spikes.
  2. Unplug High-Value Tech During Outages: When an outage occurs, unplug sensitive electronics like gaming PCs, OLED TVs, and game consoles to shield them from voltage spikes when power is restored.
  3. Refrigeration Safety: Keep refrigerator doors closed. An unopened refrigerator preserves cold temperatures for up to 4 hours; a full freezer maintains temperature for approximately 48 hours.
