Who is Woody Marks? Early Career & Rise

Born Jo'quavious Marks on December 21, 2001, in Atlanta, Georgia, "Woody" earned his nickname early in life and quickly made a name for himself at Carver High School. As a four-star recruit coming out of Georgia, Marks dominated high school competition with incredible acceleration, fluid change of direction, and natural pass-catching abilities.

Recruited heavily across the country, Marks chose Mississippi State, entering an environment that would uniquely sharpen his skillset under the late, great coach Mike Leach.

Quick Player Profile

  • Full Name: Jo'quavious "Woody" Marks
  • Position: Running Back (RB)
  • Height: 5'10"
  • Weight: 210 lbs
  • College Teams: Mississippi State Bulldogs (2020–2023), USC Trojans (2024)
  • Key Trait: Elite receiving out of the backfield & high-level pass protection

The Mississippi State Era: Setting SEC Records

When Woody Marks arrived in Starkville in 2020, Mike Leach’s signature "Air Raid" offense was in full swing. In this system, running backs are tasked with functioning as short-area receivers, safety valves, and quick-rhythm playmakers.

Marks did not just adapt—he excelled. Across four seasons with the Bulldogs, Marks set the Mississippi State program record for career receptions by a running back and became one of the most prolific pass-catching backs in Southeastern Conference (SEC) history.

Key Accomplishments at Mississippi State:

  • Recorded a catch in every single game he played for the Bulldogs (40 consecutive games).
  • Accumulating over 200 receptions while maintaining a steady rushing presence.
  • Developing top-tier blitz pickup mechanics against physical SEC defenses.

Despite playing in a heavily pass-first system, Marks maintained impressive efficiency on the ground, setting up his next major step: demonstrating he could handle a heavy rushing workload.

The USC Transfer: Broadening His Game

Ahead of his final college season, Marks entered the transfer portal and committed to Southern California (USC) under head coach Lincoln Riley. The move was strategic: Marks aimed to prove to NFL evaluators that he was not merely an Air Raid satellite back, but a true featured, between-the-tackles ball carrier.

At USC, Marks unlocked a new level of his game. Playing in both the Big Ten and against out-of-conference powerhouse programs, Marks showed off physical running between the tackles, patient vision in zone schemes, and home-run explosive speed.

His breakout season in Los Angeles solidified his reputation as a complete 3-down back, proving he could handle 15–20 carries a game while remaining a lethal receiving weapon on 3rd downs.

How Woody Marks Transformed Modern Backfield Play

Modern football demands that running backs do more than just handoffs on 1st and 2nd down. The shift toward modern spread systems, heavy nickel defenses, and pre-snap motion requires backs who can split out wide, read coverages, and pick up pass rushes.

Woody Marks serves as a blueprint for the 2026 tailback:

1. Motion & Alignment Flexibility

At USC, Lincoln Riley frequently motioned Marks out to receiver positions. Defenses matching him with linebackers were instantly at a disadvantage due to Marks's crisp route running on slant, angle, and wheel routes.

2. High-Level Blitz Recognition

Because Marks played four seasons in high-volume passing environments, he processes defensive front adjustments faster than most rookie or second-year players. He effectively communicates with the quarterback to slide protection or pick up incoming corner/safety blitzes.
